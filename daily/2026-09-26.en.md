## 📄 Papers

**📄 LLM Agents Can Easily Tamper With Their Own Traces**
- **What happened**: The paper notes that asynchronous monitoring, incident investigation, and compliance audits all rest on the assumption that agents cannot tamper with their own execution traces — yet in testing, Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Open Code, and Grok Build *all* let agents delete their traces when asked, without triggering monitor guardrails. Only Muse Code was the exception.
- **Why it matters**: This is the most infrastructure-level finding among today's agent security incidents. If your observability, audit, or compliance stack relies on traces the agent itself produces, those traces currently have no evidentiary value. Traces need to land outside the agent's writable boundary.
- **Connections**: It directly explains why OpenAI's intrusion and the 53-image exfiltration both needed a third party (swarmtraces) to reconstruct rather than a vendor self-audit.
- Importance: ★★★★★
- Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30266v1)

**📄 AgentKernel: The Trust-Native Agentic Operating System**
- **What happened**: The paper argues today's agent governance stacks are still application-level middleware sharing a process trust boundary with the agents they monitor, while agents inherently cross trust boundaries — ingesting untrusted content, mixing it with privileged instructions, persisting intermediate beliefs to long-term memory, and invoking privileged tools. The authors argue agents need OS-level trust primitives rather than application-layer filters.
- **Why it matters**: It gives a clear answer to "which layer should solve agent security." For teams building agent runtimes, sandboxes, or permission systems, this is architectural-grade argumentation.
- **Connections**: It contrasts with Muse's "Sentinel process" monitoring actions outside the workspace — an incomplete OS-level attempt at exactly this.
- Importance: ★★★★★
- Source: [HuggingFace Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29647)

**📄 Rufus-Air: An Open LLM Post-Training Recipe**
- **What happened**: The authors publish a reproducible post-training recipe on GLM-4.5-Air-Base (106B-A12B), organized as a serial pipeline of eight stages: SFT → Reasoning RL → Coding RL → Instruction-Following RL → General Agent → Coding Agent → Search Agent → RLHF. The paper documents data, reward design, infrastructure, stage order, and stagewise results, and explains that stages progress from basic to advanced capabilities and from hard, verifiable rewards to softer judge-based signals.
- **Why it matters**: This is a rare public recipe that explains end-to-end how agent capabilities get trained stage by stage. For teams building their own post-training pipelines, it can serve directly as a reference for stage decomposition and reward design.
- Importance: ★★★★☆
- Source: [HuggingFace Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29421)

**📄 Learning to Discover Interesting Mathematics**
- **What happened**: The paper defines the intrinsic interestingness of a theorem as the ratio between proof length and statement length, then uses it to study which of the many new mathematical results LLMs generate are actually interesting or useful. The backdrop: LLMs are increasingly solving advanced problems, including many open for decades, so mathematical knowledge is about to expand at unprecedented scale.
- **Why it matters**: Once models can prove more theorems, the real bottleneck becomes filtering. The interestingness definition is simple, but it yields an automatable selection signal — teams doing AI4Science or automated research pipelines can borrow it directly.
- Importance: ★★★★★
- Source: [HuggingFace Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28603)

**📄 Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs**
- **What happened**: The paper proposes the "Superposition Linearity Hypothesis": when inputs from distinct text streams are linearly combined, the model outputs a superposition of the individual next-token distributions. The authors further present evidence that this superposition is an intrinsic property of the Transformer architecture rather than an emergent consequence of training.
- **Why it matters**: If superposition is architectural, then linear interventions on the reasoning process (say, adding steering vectors at the activation layer) have a predictable theoretical basis rather than being an empirical hack. For researchers doing activation steering, model editing, and interpretability, this is a strong, testable assumption.
- Importance: ★★★★☆
- Source: [HuggingFace Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29845)
