## 📄 Papers

**📄 OmniScope: Modality-Decoupled Token Compression for Omnimodal LLMs**
- **What happened**: Existing token compression for omnimodal models typically lets one modality dictate what to keep in others — an assumption that often breaks: for the same query, audio and video relevance peak at different moments. OmniScope anchors on the query and compresses each modality independently, training-free.
- **Why it matters**: The cost bottleneck of long-video/multimodal contexts is token count. OmniScope's training-free approach can plug directly into existing inference pipelines — a low-effort, high-value engineering direction.
- Importance: ★★★★★
- Source: [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.23193)

**📄 β-OPSD: Deriving with Policy Optimization, Training with Self-Distillation**
- **What happened**: The paper identifies a structural source of OPSD's brittleness: vanilla OPSD is precisely the β=1 member of a broader policy-optimization family, where β weights the KL penalty anchoring the student to a reference policy. Making β a controllable regularizer markedly improves training stability.
- **Why it matters**: Reasoning-model training stability is one of the biggest engineering pain points in RL post-training. This equivalence turns murky hyperparameter tuning into a knob with theoretical coordinates.
- Importance: ★★★★★
- Source: [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28582)

**📄 BM25 Wins at Scale: A Scaling Study of RAG Paradigms**
- **What happened**: A controlled study compares lexical, dense, graph-based, and agentic retrieval across 28 strictly nested corpus tiers spanning ~450x in scale. The verdict is blunt: under one reader and one question set, BM25 lexical retrieval shows striking cost-performance advantages as scale grows.
- **Why it matters**: GraphRAG has been overhyped in recent years. This paper provides empirical coordinates for when graph retrieval is genuinely worth it — a contrarian but data-solid reference for teams choosing RAG architectures.
- **Connections**: VentureBeat's same-day article "Stop graphing everything" on when GraphRAG actually beats vector RAG corroborates the finding.
- Importance: ★★★★★
- Source: [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26497)

**📄 Filesystem-Based Memory for LLM Agents: Organization, Evolution, and Sustainability**
- **What happened**: Deployed agents increasingly keep long-term memory as a directory tree of markdown files they read, write, and reorganize themselves. The paper is the first to systematically test the default's two working assumptions: whether an agent can keep a growing store organized as memories accumulate, conflict, and go stale — and whether that organization scales sustainably.
- **Why it matters**: Memory is the core of long-term agent value, yet most teams over-engineer bespoke memory representations while ignoring the most primitive file-system approach. This paper provides first evidence for "start with the filesystem before building complex memory."
- **Connections**: Contrasts with Meta's memory coach and Tencent's Agent-Memory — three teams attacking the same problem from different angles.
- Importance: ★★★★★
- Source: [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26637)

**📄 See2Think: Do Multimodal Models Really Use Intermediate Visual States?**
- **What happened**: The paper introduces a unified evaluation framework that diagnoses how intermediate visual states (sketches, annotations, tool outputs) are generated, rendered, and used during multimodal reasoning. Existing benchmarks either cover narrow tasks or are partially solvable via text alone, and only score final answers without analyzing the process.
- **Why it matters**: More models reason with self-generated visuals, yet "does the model actually depend on what it draws" has no answer. The framework offers directly transferable lessons for evaluation design.
- Importance: ★★★★★
- Source: [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26769)
